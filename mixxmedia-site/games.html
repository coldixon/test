How we make games

Three rules we don’t break.

Inklings was designed by an eight-year-old and a ten-year-old who had to play it every night to know whether it was any good. That shaped how we build everything since.

Rule one

Fun first, learning underneath

If a child would rather do something else, the learning never happens. The maths in our games is load-bearing, but it is never the reason a kid picks it up.

Rule two

Everyone is in the deck

Our founders made a point of designing a cast that looked like every table it would land on. That has been a design requirement ever since, not a marketing afterthought.

Rule three

Playtest with actual children

Rules that sound elegant on paper fall apart at a kitchen table in about four minutes. We would rather find that out early.

Meet the cast

Inky, the Lightning King, and the dancing panda.

A deck of 54 cards is really a cast of characters, drawn by a kid who taught herself to draw specifically so this game could exist.

Inky is the one everyone fears — the single card in the deck that nothing can block. The Duck Shields are the only defense worth holding onto. And the characters players tell us they love most are the ones the shorts will start with.
